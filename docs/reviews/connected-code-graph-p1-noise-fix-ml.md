**Verdict: approve with suggestions** (0 blockers, 3 majors, 4 minors). Option A is a defensible rule with a real, class-level reason and the t2 arithmetic reproduces. The majors are about what the gate result will and will not mean: the held-out cost is understated by the plan's own estimate, the dev concordance gain is manufactured by the cut, and the fix loop has no recorded bound. All are cheap before R3 is committed.

**Falsifiable stopping condition (the fix loop; to be written into the R3 record or its note before F is cut).** t3 is the last tree for dev noise. Proceed to the freeze-check and spend only if, at t3, labelled Q <= 4 on the point estimate for both pooled and scripts, **and** the lost dev hits are exactly the four named items (no other dev item newly unlisted), **and** t3 pooled Q lies inside the replay interval [1.49, 5.50] (a value outside it says the offline replay did not predict the walk, so the cost model is also void). Otherwise stop: no further engine change motivated by labelled dev noise before held-out; the stakeholder chooses between deferring criterion 3 to P2 and not spending. Any engine rule found on the t2/t3 labels counts as a second fix and is excluded by this sentence. The rule is falsified by a t3 pooled or scripts Q above 4, by a fifth lost dev hit, or by a t3 pooled Q outside the interval.

## Findings

### M1 (major): the held-out cost estimate leaves out a scored class; the likely loss is about 2 items, not 1

The plan (§4) counts 1 coincident hit in 47 unflagged non-key-error scripts items and reads about 1 expected held-out item, chance of at least one near 0.6. Held-out key-error analogues are **scored as misses** (prereg §3, key-error precedent), so a held-out analogue that lists only through a prose sibling is a hit today and a miss after A, exactly like the "none" class. Dev has 2 such items among the 50 unflagged counting scripts items (`7fc6a23c` none; `f7a50995` key-error), not 1 of 47. The withheld shapes are not scored, so the 2 flagged coincident items rightly drop out. Reading: rate 2/50, about 2 expected over ~50 scored held-out scripts items, P(at least one) about 0.86 under the plan's own Poisson model; the exact (Garwood) 95% interval on the count of 2 is [0.24, 7.2] events per dev-sized set, i.e. P(at least one) anywhere from about 0.2 to 0.999. The interval is the finding: the data do not pin it. Two biases push the held-out rate up, not down: dev concordance (50 of 54) was engineered by resolver work done on dev items, and the clustering of the 4 coincident hits (3 clusters or fewer, unverified) makes the effective sample smaller than 54.

Consequence for the verdict: the held-out claim is "every scored item a hit", a zero-miss rule. Baseline clean-pass at c = 52 is 0.35 at r = 0.98 and 0.21 at 0.97 (prereg §6, arithmetic). Lowering r by the coincident rate (about 0.02 to 0.04) moves the 0.98 case to roughly 0.07 to 0.13: a factor of 3 to 5 less likely to pass, labelled estimate, no measurement. The spend is once-only (re-gate needs fresh clusters, floor 35). The dev noise criterion A satisfies is **not part of the claim** ("noise not measured"). Put to the stakeholder as: A buys a dev criterion that the claim never cites, at an expected 3-to-5-fold cut in the chance the one held-out spend passes. The recommended answer is still A if the stakeholder wants the dev criterion met (no other candidate in §3 reaches 4 without losing the same 3 to 4 hits, and D, the only hit-free one, ends at 4.86 / 4.37), but it must be asked with this number, not with "about 0.6", and the alternative "defer criterion 3 to P2, spend on the t2 tree" must be on the table. The held-out verdict stays the claimed one (listing only, noise not measured) but describes an engine whose recall was lowered by a precision-motivated rule; the claim text must say so (see M2, m4).

### M2 (major): the dev concordance gain (50 of 50) is manufactured by the cut, and the claim rule would read it as a resolver result

The prereg writes "the scripts resolver recovers cross-script links" only over concordant hits, and not at all if any scripts hit is coincident-only. After A the coincident hits cannot exist: their listing route (a D2 prose-sibling chain) is cut, so they become not-listed items (2 withheld, 1 key-error, 1 unclassified). Concordant "50 of 50" then reads as a resolver strength it does not show; the four items were not resolved, they were removed. Required: the R3 report states hits as raw counts (scripts 50 of 64, not 54 of 64 at t2) beside the entry-criterion arithmetic (50 + 10 + 3 + 1), states that the concordance change is caused by A, and the claim sentence about cross-script links stays unwritten at P1 unless the held-out concordance is shown independently of A. The plan's sentence "the record's claim rule no longer blocks the sentence on dev" should be dropped.

### M3 (major): the fix loop is unbounded in the record; the tree ordinal resets the visible count

Prereg §5 ("largest across reruns at the tree", a rerun reuses the seed and the labels, unchanged pairs reuse t2 labels at a new tree) closes the within-tree reroll and the same-answer relabel. It does not bound trees. At R3 the report's run count restarts at 1 while t1, t2 and this design are three exposures of the same dev items and, through label reuse, mostly the same labelled items; the t3 draw is a fresh hash sort under a new seed, so each new tree is also a new draw. The plan's §9 (a second miss is stop-and-ask) is the right instinct but lives in a plan, not the record. Adequacy for a new tree: adequate against rerolling the same tree, inadequate against fix, redraw, fix. Required: the stopping condition above, committed with R3 and cited in the report; the report states "tuning iterations: t1, t2, t3 = 3, cap 3". Also define "unchanged pair" for label reuse as identical `(anchorId, itemId, chain)`; a kept item whose best chain changed under A (the plan's own limit 2) is relabelled blind, not reused.

### m1 (minor): is A fitted to the 31 labelled items? Partly, and say so

For a general justification: a `prompt-mention` edge is a class property fixed before the labels (the extractor never marks it `confirmed`; it asserts no call, data or control dependence), and "a prose mention is a sound first hop and a weak second hop" is a product-semantic rule that would hold on any repository's prose. That part is independent of dev. Against it: (a) the argument "doubt stacked on doubt" proves too much; it applies to every `possible` hop and is option C, which the plan rejects on **dev labels** (5 of 20 shell items real). So the narrowing to the mention class was chosen by the labels. (b) The rule was selected from about six candidates scanned against the same labels (`opts.py`); a mild winner's-curse on the point estimate. (c) The bound value 1 is the only value that changes anything at `defaultDepthBound` 2. (d) The gold disagrees at the margin: the 4 lost hits are gold-real items reached only through D2 prose chains, so the class is not 0% real. "0 of 31" is rubric-real, not gold-real, and 31 items are not 31 independent observations: the 19 labelled scripts items in the class sit in 10 anchors and 13 distinct mention-hop targets (all 230 frame items: 14 anchors, 43 targets). Wilson 95% upper bound on the real share in the class: 0.11 at n = 31, 0.17 at 19, 0.23 at 13 targets; the gold lower bound is 4 of 230 = 1.7%. Reading: A is a principled class rule whose harmlessness evidence is thinner and more clustered than the plan's 0.11. Label it a refit rule (as §9 does) and state the cluster-adjusted bound. The "est -2 real" in the table is an estimator artefact, not evidence of zero cost: real removed is at least 4 (gold), likely 2 to 25.

**Reweighting versus removal (recomputed, matches the plan).** Pooled Q 4.779 to 2.873; scripts 4.101 to 1.985. Of the 232 false items removed (W-tilde 596.2 to 364.2), 202 are strata wholly inside the rule (prompt-mention band 2, 198, labelled 23 of 23 false; name-match band 2, 4, census), 28 are the shell-dynamic band-2 items whose chain holds a mention hop (4 labelled, all false), and about 2 are the estimator shifting the kept shell-dynamic stratum rate (15 of 20 false before; 11 of 16 after), which also adds 2 to R-tilde. With that shift removed Q is 2.94 pooled instead of 2.87. If the 28 had the stratum-average rate (3 of 4 false rather than 4 of 4), the kept stratum reads 15 of 20 and Q is 3.17 pooled, 2.20 scripts. So the gain is removal of labelled-false items, not reweighting, with a robustness range 2.9 to 3.2 pooled. The Q after A is a direct estimate on the kept strata and does not depend on the real share of the dropped class; that share matters for the recall cost (M1), not for Q. Interval: the plan's [1.49, 5.50] pooled and [0.90, 4.35] scripts reproduce within bootstrap noise (my B = 2000: [1.51, 5.55] and [0.86, 4.48]). Pooled band 1 (3.24) is unchanged by A and carries most of the remaining variance: prompt-mention band 1 is 22 of 24 false over 208 items.

### m2 (minor): chance that t3 is again above 4

The plan's 16% pooled (4% scripts) is a bootstrap over answers with the t2 labels held fixed. The t3 draw also resamples the labels. My posterior-predictive simulation over the same strata (Jeffreys prior on each stratum's false rate, then the t3 allocation and a hypergeometric draw, the t3 frame of 461 pooled and 310 scripts possible items) gives P(Q > 4) about 0.08 pooled and 0.04 scripts for draw-and-rate variation alone (0.05 with a uniform prior). The two sources overlap and neither contains clustering of the prompt-mention band-1 rate; I read the combined chance as about 0.10 to 0.20 pooled and at most 0.10 scripts, with the pooled figure the binding one. A t3 miss is a live outcome, as §9 says; the stopping condition above is what keeps a miss from becoming a third fix.

### m3 (minor): label versus gold, and what to disclose (question 2)

Keep the rubric and the labels for the gate: changing the measurement after the result is the tuning this gate forbids, and the direction of the change (toward passing) is the suspicious one. Two corrections to the plan's framing. First, the sensitivity (gold-matched items counted real) puts the t2 baseline already under the bar (pooled 3.24, scripts 3.75): the t2 miss is conditional on the rubric, not on the engine alone, and the stakeholder should read that figure before approving a recall-cutting engine rule. After A the same sensitivity reads 1.90 and 1.86. Second, the gold-matched evidence "8 of 10 false against 14 of 14 false" is two-sided Fisher p about 0.16: it shows the labeller applying the rubric uniformly, not a different treatment of gold items; the spot-check (19 of 20, 20 of 20) is the same: label stability, not validity. The substantive tension is construct: Q is measured against "no runtime dependency", recall against a gold that counts prose documents (all 36 mcp and 28 of 54 scripts hits are reached only through a D1 mention). The engine can be 100% on the gold and above 4 on Q by construction, and any Q fix cuts prose chains the gold counts. Disclose in the R3 report and the claim: the two constructs, the sensitivity figures for baseline and post-A beside the gate figure (never in its place), the 4 lost gold-real items, and that A is the design resolving the tension in the rubric's favour at the gold's expense. Decide the rubric for P2's measured gate, as the plan says.

### m4 (minor): the 0 of 31 evidence cannot be re-measured after the fix, and the cost side has no audit

At t3 the dropped items are no longer in the frame, so the labelling never revisits the class A removed. The only cost evidence is the t2 sample. Cheap addition before R3: label a further disjoint draw of 30 to 40 from the 199 unsampled dropped items (offline from the saved frame, blind, same rubric, same labeller role), reported as the cost audit. Not a gate; it tightens the clustered 0.11 to 0.23 bound above and costs no engine or freeze change.

### n1 (nit): wording and test coverage

Drop "est -2" from the table (see m1). Add to §7 a test that the cut applies to a hop reached in the depends-on column through a `prompt-mention` increment-1 row, and one where the same anchor reaches the same item by a second non-mention chain at distance 2 (non-dominated arrival kept, item still listed): the plan's limit 2 names the case but no test pins it.

## Question 5: `7fc6a23c` (entry-criterion item 1)

Judgment: confirm as a fourth dev key-error (option a), under conditions. It meets the existing key-error definition independent of A: a cwd-relative path the resolver deliberately does not place (CO-2c removed such edges on purpose), the same family as the confirmed `f7a50995`, which was already listed only through a prose sibling at t2. Rejected: (b) a new shape letter edits the pinned predicate and withholds on a ground that arises from the fix, not from a documented engine limit; (c) reverses CO-2c and cannot be measured offline, a second engine change on the same loop; (d) leaves the entry criterion unmet by a cause already known, which blocks the spend on a bookkeeping item. Conditions: the ruling is the stakeholder's, made before R3 (the key-error record's sha is pinned in the record, so the record is rebuilt anyway), its recorded reason cites CO-2c and the definition, not Q or A; the report states that its status changed because A removed its only listing route; the raw dev hit count is reported beside the criterion (50 of 64, 78%, against 54 of 64 at t2) because with a growing key-error list the item-1 criterion can no longer fail and carries no information about recall; held-out analogues stay scored as misses and enter the M1 estimate.
