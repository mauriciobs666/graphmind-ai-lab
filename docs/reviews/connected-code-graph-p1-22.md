## Findings

### F1 (blocker, verified by repro) — an item's `chain` can disagree with the item's own cost: stale predecessor links under the depth bound
`bfs.py:728` stores `best[key] = Entry(cost, frm, edge)` with `frm` a **key**; `Walk.chain` (`bfs.py:564-572`) resolves `best[pred]` **at the end**. When a state is later improved on `(possible, distance)` its successor's improvement is cut by the bound (`bfs.py:714`, increment 1 past the bound), so the successor keeps its old record but its chain now runs through the predecessor's *new* best. Planted graph (12 nodes, `A`←`c1`…, a short `name-match` route to `Gs` at (1,1) arriving before a longer confirmed route at (0,2); `c4` calls `Gs`): item `H` reports `distance 2, possible_hops 1, status possible`, but its chain has **9 hops, all `confirmed`, three `CALLS` hops (distance 3)**. Downstream: V7 (status/reason follow hops) and the distance recompute reject the answer built from it, so a legitimate question becomes unanswerable at P1-39, or P1-23 papers over it. Random graphs do not trigger it (0 mismatches in 4,452 items, bound 2): it needs the improved-then-cut topology.
Fix (run, not just argued): make `Entry.pred` the predecessor's **`Entry` object captured at expansion time** (`best[k]` read when the candidate is made, before the next-level loop replaces entries) and have `Walk.chain` follow entry objects. In a scratch copy that gives `H`: 5 hops, `CALLS_NAME` possible first, distance 2, and the 252 engine/equivalence tests stay green.
Test: the planted graph above asserting `item.possible_hops == #possible edges in chain` and `item.distance ==` the sum of the chain rows' increments. Coverage probe over the axes, not a list of shapes: arrival order (possible route shorter / longer than the confirmed route) × bound (state at / below it) × increment of the successor edge (0 / 1) × column. The existing random harness cannot catch this class (it passes today); keep a recompute-from-chain invariant in it as well.

### F2 (major, verified by repro) — bounded reach is order-dependent: an item reachable within the bound by a `possible` route is listed or dropped depending only on route *length*
The best entry is lexicographic `(possible, distance)` (`bfs.py:725`), so a state first reached at (1,1) and later at (0,2) keeps (0,2) and is "re-expanded" with distance 2 — its increment-1 edges are then cut. Whether the earlier (1,1) expansion already produced the successor decides the outcome. Two graphs identical except the length of the `name-match` route (`probe_pareto2`): short route → `H` listed (possible, d 2, and F1's bad chain); long route → `H` absent and `unexplored = 1`, although `H` lies at distance 2 by the possible route (the all-confirmed twin lists it at 2). Spec §4.1 says items are at distance ≤ bound; §4.2 defines `E_cut` from "the final best set". Neither says which of the two outcomes is right, and the engine gives both. Unbounded the rule is exact (monotone additive lexicographic cost, termination: each re-expansion strictly decreases a pair in ℕ×ℕ; 60 slice-oracle graphs and 4,490 random walks show 0 differences against reachable-within-bound), so this is bound-specific.
Needs a spec ruling, recommendation: re-expand also when `distance` alone is strictly smaller (Pareto on distance; still terminates, distance strictly decreases), keep the lexicographic entry only for the *displayed* chain, and count `E_cut` from the final best set as §4.2 says. Assertion that would catch a wrong design: the long-route/short-route twin pair must list the same items, and the all-confirmed twin's items must be a subset of the bounded walk's. I have not implemented the Pareto variant; hand the design to the implementer with that assertion.

### F3 (major, verified by repro; needs a ruling) — a `ConfigEntry` anchor lists consumer sites the entry does not bind
`_bound_in` (`bfs.py:383-389`) drops `b.siteId`/`b.ord` that the read returns (`-p1-graph.md:220`: "config change → the sites it binds"; G7: `BOUND_BY` is per consumer site), and `bound_in` → `Link`(`value`) → `via_in:value` lists **every** `VIA` site of the link. Planted: link `L` with sites `s1`,`s2`, entry `E` bound only for `s1`; walk from `E` lists `C1` **and `C2`** (`probe_cfg`). `C2`'s binding is some other entry or none, so `E`'s change does not reach it: a false `confirmed`/link-status item. The unit test (`test_a_config_change_reaches_the_consumers_of_the_links_it_binds…`) pins the over-list, so it cannot fail on this. Graph note §6.2 ("the links it binds → their consumers") and `-p1-graph.md` §5 ("the sites it binds") disagree; G7 is the later, P1-specific one.
Fix: carry the bound `siteId` into the `Link` state reached from a config entry and have `via_in` list only that site; or, if the ruling is "all consumers of the link", amend the `-p1-graph.md` §5 gloss and rename the pin so it states the choice. Test: the planted graph (C2 absent), and an entry bound at two sites listing exactly those two.

### The delegate's 8 argued-equivalent survivors (differential fuzz: 3,069 walks plain and 3,069 with every reader reply shuffled per mutant, comparing items with chains, best chain per state, arrivals, cut set, levels and frontier sizes against the original; the original itself agrees with itself across plain/shuffled on 6,408 walks)
Equivalent, each with a reason I checked (no distinguishing graph found, and one cannot exist):
- `dom.cuts-from-best-only`, 0 diffs: non-dominated arrivals have distinct distances, so only the largest-distance one — the lexicographic best — can sit at the bound and carry cuts.
- `dom.frontier-unfiltered`, 0 diffs including `frontier_sizes`: candidates of one key are inserted in cost order and a dominator sorts before the arrival it dominates, so a dominated arrival never enters `nxt` (the filter is dead given the sort; it is defence in depth).
- `items.only-best`, `items.unsorted`, `items.rank-drops-edge`: an arrival's item-ness is fixed by its state key (every arrival at one state shares the edge class), the rank is a total order through `k`, and two kept arrivals at one state never share a cost, so the edge term cannot decide.
- `sort.keys-unsorted`: states are independent; the frontier is re-sorted in `_expand`.
- `sort.entries-sort-drops-cost`: equivalent **only while `own == possible hops`**; P1-23's split makes it live, so its brief should carry a test.

### New findings
**N1 (minor, verified) — the new `site` state is outside the answer schema's hop `state` enum.** `ccg/schemas/answer-v1.schema.json:1441-1450` allows `value, path, function, contract, key, end`; a config-anchor chain's `Link` hop carries `("site", siteId)` (`state.py`, `bfs.py` `bound_in` → `site`), and spec2 does not say how it is reported. P1-39's builder would emit an invalid hop or invent a mapping. Fix: one sentence in spec2 §3.3 (`Link` hop from a config entry reports `value`; the site restriction is internal) or an additive enum value with its V-rule, decided before P1-39; add a builder-seam test.
**N2 (minor, verified by mutation) — the equal-cost tie-break by predecessor key is unpinned.** Dropping the sort of a key's candidates (`for … in cands[key]:` for `sorted(cands[key], key=c[:3])`) survives all tests but changes the displayed chain in 9 of 2,550 plain walks and 183 of 2,550 shuffled-reply walks (e.g. `random_world(36)`, anchor 0, bound 2: state `Symbol s1 / path a0` shows a different, equal-cost chain). The cost order ends "then the predecessor's stable key" (spec2 §1.2), so the shown chain is spec-visible and FR-19 deterministic only through that sort. The oracle compares items by `(possible, distance)` only, so it cannot see it. Fix: a test that plants two equal-pair arrivals whose reader/insertion order is opposite to their stable-key order (axes: pred label × pred kind × reader order, run under the shuffled-reply harness) asserting the smaller key's chain.

### Fixes against each other, and the oracle
Interactions checked: site state × dominance (state key includes the site, so per-site arrivals are separate; at most `bound+1` arrivals per state, so termination holds); `not_via` removed × wiring (`wiring` reads `value`/`contract` only, a `Link` entered in `site` state cannot walk back to its entry — `test_…lists_no_wiring_entry_back` kills the `site_off` mutant); dominance × `unexplored` (cuts from kept arrivals, proven equal to cuts from the best); dominance × F1 chain (items use their own arrival's chain); re-pinned definition × token validation (unknown tokens raise, record changed once); Link-anchor rejection × wiring/`to_out` (their Link-facts read is unreachable from an anchor). Only N1 is a seam.
The new oracle is independent in algorithm (a fixed point over `(state, distance) → min possible hops`, not the dominance walk) and covers items, costs and `unexplored` at bounds 1-3 over 120 worlds; it shares `bfs._expand`, so **the row semantics are not oracled by it**. What covers them: the literal pin table (independent of `ROWS`), the behaviour tests (85/85 above with the pin tests removed), the slice's own `impact.py` oracle for the slice rows, and my `_expand`-level mutants (`site_off`, arg-path, match-path). Residual: chain choice among equal costs is outside the oracle (N2).
